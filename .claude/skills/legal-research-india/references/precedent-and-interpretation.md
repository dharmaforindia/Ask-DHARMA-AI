# Precedent and statutory interpretation

Items marked [VERIFY] are from memory and need checking before being cited.

## 1. Rules of precedent in India

- **Art 141:** the law declared by the Supreme Court binds all courts in India. **Art 144:** all
  authorities must act in aid of the Supreme Court.
- **Bench strength:** a decision of a larger bench binds a smaller bench. A bench of equal strength
  that disagrees with an earlier coordinate bench should refer the question to a larger bench rather
  than decline to follow it [VERIFY: *Central Board of Dawoodi Bohra Community v. State of
  Maharashtra*, (2005) 2 SCC 673].
- **Conflicting decisions of coordinate benches:** follow the larger bench. Where the benches are of
  equal strength, the position on which to follow is not uniform; check the latest guidance and
  explain both decisions to the court [VERIFY].
- **High Courts:** a High Court's decisions bind the courts subordinate to it. Within the High
  Court, a single judge is bound by a Division Bench, and a Division Bench by a Full Bench. A single
  judge who disagrees with another single judge should refer the question to a larger bench.
  Decisions of other High Courts are persuasive only.
- **Ratio decidendi:** the principle necessary for the decision on the material facts. A decision
  is an authority for what it actually decides, not for every observation in it, nor for what may
  logically seem to follow from it [VERIFY: *State of Orissa v. Sudhansu Sekhar Misra*, AIR 1968 SC
  647].
- **Obiter dicta:** a considered observation of the Supreme Court, even if obiter, is generally
  followed by High Courts. A casual observation carries less weight.
- **Per incuriam:** a decision given in ignorance of a binding statute or binding precedent is not a
  binding precedent on that point.
- **Sub silentio:** a point not argued or considered is not decided by implication.
- **Dismissal of an SLP in limine** by a non-speaking order declares no law under Art 141
  (*Kunhayammed v. State of Kerala*, 19 July 2000, three judges; reported as (2000) 6 SCC 359
  [VERIFY the SCC citation]).
- **Prospective overruling** is available only to the Supreme Court and is applied sparingly.
- **Pre-1950 decisions** of the Privy Council and Federal Court remain binding on High Courts
  unless the Supreme Court has departed from them.

## 2. How to distinguish an authority

1. **Different material facts.** Show the fact the ratio depended on, and that it is absent here.
2. **Different statute or version.** The provision was amended, or the case was under a different
   Act with different words.
3. **The point was not argued or decided** (sub silentio).
4. **Per incuriam.** It ignored a binding provision or a larger-bench decision.
5. **Overtaken.** A later larger bench or an amendment changed the law.
6. **Obiter.** The observation was not necessary to the decision.
7. **Concession-based.** The result followed a concession by counsel, not a decision of the court.

Never misrepresent what an authority says in order to distinguish it.

## 3. Statutory interpretation

**Basic rules:**

| Rule | Use |
|---|---|
| Literal (plain meaning) | The starting point. Where the words are clear, give them their ordinary meaning |
| Golden rule | Depart from the literal meaning only to avoid an absurdity or inconsistency |
| Mischief rule (*Heydon's case*) | Ask what mischief the law was meant to cure, and read it to suppress the mischief and advance the remedy |
| Purposive construction | Read the words in light of the statute's object, especially for social welfare laws |
| Harmonious construction | Read provisions that appear to conflict so that both can operate |
| Read the statute as a whole | Sections take colour from the scheme of the Act, the chapter and the definitions |

**Aids and maxims:**

- *Ejusdem generis:* general words after specific words of one kind are limited to that kind.
- *Noscitur a sociis:* a word takes its meaning from the words around it.
- *Expressio unius est exclusio alterius:* expressly mentioning one thing excludes others.
- *Generalia specialibus non derogant:* a special provision prevails over a general one on its
  subject.
- *Ut res magis valeat quam pereat:* prefer the reading that makes the provision work.
- **Non-obstante clauses** ("notwithstanding anything contained in ...") give the provision
  overriding effect over what they name.
- **Provisos** carve out an exception from the main provision and do not usually enlarge it.
  **Explanations** clarify the main provision.
- **Definitions:** "means" is exhaustive; "includes" extends the ordinary meaning.
- **"Shall" and "may":** "shall" is usually mandatory and "may" directory, but the context,
  the consequence of non-compliance and the object decide.
- **Headings, marginal notes, the preamble and the Statement of Objects and Reasons** are aids
  where the words are ambiguous, not to override clear words.
- **Legislative history:** Law Commission reports and parliamentary debates may be used to
  understand the mischief, with caution.

**Presumptions by type of law:**

- **Penal statutes** are construed strictly. Any genuine ambiguity is resolved in favour of the
  accused.
- **Taxing statutes** are construed strictly. There is no tax by intendment, and an exemption
  provision is read strictly against the person claiming it.
- **Beneficial and welfare legislation** (labour, consumer, social security) is construed liberally
  in favour of the intended beneficiaries.
- **Procedural law** generally applies to pending proceedings; **substantive law** is presumed
  prospective unless made retrospective expressly or by necessary implication.
- **Ouster of the jurisdiction of civil courts** is not readily inferred.

**General Clauses Act, 1897 (key sections):**

- s.6: the effect of repeal; rights, liabilities and pending proceedings are saved unless a
  different intention appears.
- s.9: computing time; exclude the first day and include the last.
- s.10: if the last day falls on a day the court or office is closed, the act may be done on the
  next working day.
- s.21: a power to issue a notification or order includes the power to amend, vary or rescind it.
- s.27: service by registered post is presumed when properly addressed, prepaid and posted.

## 4. Constitutional interpretation

- **Presumption of constitutionality:** whoever challenges a law must show it is unconstitutional.
- **Reading down:** a provision may be read narrowly to save it from invalidity.
- **Severability:** the invalid part is struck down if the rest can stand on its own.
- **Pith and substance:** to decide whether a law falls within a legislative entry, look at its true
  nature and character. Incidental encroachment on another list does not invalidate it.
- **Colourable legislation:** a legislature cannot do indirectly what it cannot do directly.
- **Doctrine of eclipse** (for pre-Constitution laws inconsistent with fundamental rights).
- **Basic structure:** constitutional amendments cannot destroy the basic structure (*Kesavananda
  Bharati*, (1973) 4 SCC 225 [VERIFY]).
- **Art 14 tests:** reasonable classification (an intelligible differentia with a rational nexus to
  the object) and manifest arbitrariness (*Shayara Bano v. Union of India*, (2017) 9 SCC 1 [VERIFY]).
- **Proportionality** for restrictions on fundamental rights: a legitimate aim, a rational
  connection, necessity (the least restrictive measure) and balancing [VERIFY: *Modern Dental
  College v. State of M.P.*, (2016) 7 SCC 353; *K.S. Puttaswamy*, (2017) 10 SCC 1].
