# Criminal pleadings and applications

New sections first (BNSS, BNS, BSA), old sections in brackets. For offences committed before
1 July 2024, the IPC applies. For proceedings pending on that date, the CrPC applies (s.531 BNSS).
Regular bail, anticipatory bail and quashing are in `core-litigation.md`. Items marked [VERIFY] need
checking against the bare Act.

---

## 1. Private complaint (s.223 BNSS, old s.200 CrPC)

```
IN THE COURT OF THE ___ JUDICIAL MAGISTRATE FIRST CLASS / ACMM, AT ___
P.C.R. NO. ___ OF 20__

COMPLAINANT: ___          ACCUSED: 1. ___ 2. ___

PRIVATE COMPLAINT UNDER SECTION 223 OF THE BNSS FOR OFFENCES PUNISHABLE UNDER SECTIONS ___ OF
THE BNS

1. The complainant: ___. The accused: ___ (relationship to the complainant, role of each).
2. Facts in date order, with documents: ___.
3. The offences: Section ___ BNS requires [ingredients]. These are made out because ___
   (one paragraph per offence, ingredient by ingredient).
4. Earlier approach to the police, if any: ___ (date of complaint to the SHO and SP; result).
5. Jurisdiction: the offence was committed at ___, within this Court's jurisdiction.
6. Limitation: within time under s.514 BNSS (old s.468 CrPC), or not applicable.
7. List of witnesses: ___.   List of documents: ___.

PRAYER: Take cognizance of the offences under sections ___ BNS, record the sworn statement of the
complainant and witnesses, and issue process against the accused.
VERIFICATION.
```

Notes: The Magistrate examines the complainant on oath. Under the BNSS, the Magistrate must give
the accused an opportunity to be heard before taking cognizance (first proviso to s.223(1)). Where
the accused lives outside the Magistrate's area, an inquiry under s.225 BNSS (old s.202 CrPC) is
needed before process issues [VERIFY].

## 2. Complaint under s.138 Negotiable Instruments Act

```
COMPLAINT UNDER SECTIONS 138 AND 142 OF THE NEGOTIABLE INSTRUMENTS ACT, 1881

1. The complainant [payee / holder in due course]: ___. The accused [drawer]: ___.
   [For a company: Accused 1 is the company; Accused 2 to __ are the directors / officers in charge
   of and responsible for the conduct of its business at the relevant time (s.141). Plead each
   person's specific role. A bare repetition of the words of s.141 may not be enough.]
2. The legally enforceable debt or liability: ___ (loan / invoice / agreement dated ___).
3. Cheque no. ___ dated ___ for Rs. ___ drawn on ___ Bank, ___ branch, issued towards it.
4. Presented on ___ (within its validity); returned unpaid on ___ with the endorsement "___"
   (return memo, Annex __). The complainant learned of the dishonour on ___.
5. Demand notice dated ___ (within 30 days of that information) sent by ___; served on ___ /
   returned "___" on ___ (deemed service). The 15 days to pay ended on ___. No payment was made.
6. The cause of action arose on ___. This complaint is filed within one month of it (s.142(1)(b)).
7. Jurisdiction: the cheque was delivered for collection through the complainant's account at
   ___ Bank, ___ branch, within this Court's jurisdiction (s.142(2)(a)).
PRAYER: Take cognizance, summon the accused, try and punish them under s.138, and award
compensation under s.357 CrPC / s.395 BNSS [VERIFY the corresponding section].
[Evidence of the complainant may be given on affidavit (s.145). Consider an application for interim
compensation of up to 20% of the cheque amount (s.143A).]
```

Defence notes: the presumption under s.139 is rebuttable on a preponderance of probabilities
(*Rangappa v. Sri Mohan*, [2010] 6 SCR 507). An appellate court may order a deposit of at least 20%
of the fine or compensation (s.148).

## 3. Application to the Magistrate under s.175(3) BNSS (old s.156(3) CrPC)

```
APPLICATION UNDER SECTION 175(3) OF THE BNSS PRAYING FOR A DIRECTION TO THE SHO, ___ POLICE
STATION, TO REGISTER AN FIR AND INVESTIGATE

1. Facts disclosing cognizable offences under sections ___ BNS: ___.
2. The applicant gave information to the SHO on ___ (copy, Annex __). It was not registered.
3. The applicant then sent the substance of the information to the Superintendent of Police /
   Commissioner under s.173(4) BNSS on ___ (copy, Annex __). No action was taken.
4. This application is supported by the applicant's affidavit.
PRAYER: Direct the SHO, ___ Police Station, to register an FIR and investigate.
```

Notes: s.175(3) BNSS requires an application supported by an affidavit, and allows the Magistrate
to make an inquiry and consider the police officer's submissions. Before the BNSS, the affidavit
requirement came from *Priyanka Srivastava v. State of U.P.*, (2015) 6 SCC 287 [VERIFY].

## 4. Protest petition against a closure report

In Karnataka, a police closure report is usually called a "B" final report (case found false) or a
"C" final report (mistake of fact or a civil dispute) [VERIFY local usage]. The informant must be
given notice and heard before the Magistrate accepts a closure report [VERIFY: *Bhagwant Singh v.
Commissioner of Police*, (1985) 2 SCC 537].

```
PROTEST PETITION AGAINST THE FINAL REPORT DATED ___ IN CRIME NO. ___ OF ___ POLICE STATION

1. The investigation was perfunctory: ___ (witnesses not examined, documents not collected,
   statements ignored).
2. The material on record discloses offences under sections ___: ___.
PRAYER: Reject the final report, and (a) take cognizance on the material on record, or (b) direct
further investigation under s.193(9) BNSS (old s.173(8) CrPC), or (c) treat this petition as a
complaint and proceed under s.223 BNSS.
```

## 5. Discharge application (s.250 or s.262 BNSS, old ss.227 or 239 CrPC)

```
APPLICATION UNDER SECTION [250 / 262] OF THE BNSS FOR DISCHARGE

1. The charge-sheet and the documents produced, taken at their highest, do not disclose the
   ingredients of the offence under s.___ BNS because ___.
2. There is no material that the accused [did ___]. The statements of CW-__ and CW-__ show ___.
3. [Legal bar: no sanction under s.218 BNSS / limitation / no jurisdiction.]
PRAYER: Discharge the accused of the offences under sections ___.
```

Notes: At this stage the court looks only at the prosecution's material, to see whether there is a
ground to presume the offence (grave suspicion), and does not weigh defence evidence. The BNSS sets a
time limit of 60 days for filing a discharge application (from committal under s.250; from supply of
documents under s.262) [VERIFY].

## 6. Default bail (s.187(3) BNSS, old s.167(2) CrPC)

```
APPLICATION UNDER SECTION 187(3) OF THE BNSS FOR STATUTORY (DEFAULT) BAIL

1. The applicant was arrested on ___ and first produced before the Magistrate on ___.
2. The offence alleged is punishable with ___, so the period for investigation is [60 / 90] days,
   which ended on ___.
3. No charge-sheet has been filed as of today, ___.
4. The applicant is ready and willing to furnish bail, and avails of the right now.
PRAYER: Release the applicant on bail under s.187(3) BNSS.
```

Notes: File the application the day the period ends, before a charge-sheet is filed. The right is
lost if the charge-sheet comes first and no application was made [VERIFY: *Uday Mohanlal Acharya*,
(2001) 5 SCC 453; *M. Ravindran v. DRI*, (2021) 2 SCC 485]. Special statutes such as the NDPS Act
(s.36A(4)) and UAPA (s.43D(2)) allow extensions [VERIFY].

## 7. Cancellation of bail (s.483(3) BNSS, old s.439(2) CrPC)

Plead the bail order and its conditions; the supervening misconduct (threatening witnesses,
tampering with evidence, a fresh offence, absconding, breach of conditions) with dates and proof; or
that the order ignored relevant material or relied on irrelevant material. Cancellation of bail
already granted needs strong reasons; it is not a re-hearing of the bail application.

---

## 8. Suspension of sentence and bail pending appeal (s.430 BNSS, old s.389 CrPC)

Plead the conviction and sentence; that the appeal has been admitted; the length of sentence and
time already served; prima facie strong grounds (errors on the face of the judgment); conduct while
on bail during the trial; and the likely delay in hearing the appeal. For a fixed-term sentence,
suspension is ordinarily granted unless there are exceptional reasons against it [VERIFY].

## 9. Criminal appeal memorandum (s.415 BNSS, old s.374 CrPC)

```
MEMORANDUM OF CRIMINAL APPEAL UNDER SECTION 415(2) OF THE BNSS AGAINST THE JUDGMENT OF CONVICTION
AND ORDER ON SENTENCE DATED ___ IN S.C. / C.C. NO. ___ ON THE FILE OF ___

GROUNDS
1. The prosecution failed to prove the ingredient of ___ beyond reasonable doubt.
2. The trial court relied on the evidence of PW-__, which is full of material contradictions and
   omissions (pages ___).
3. [Circumstantial evidence] The chain is incomplete; the circumstances of ___ and ___ are not proved.
4. Recovery under s.23 BSA (old s.27 Evidence Act) was not proved: the mahazar witnesses turned
   hostile / were not independent.
5. The s.63 BSA (old s.65B Evidence Act) certificate for the electronic evidence was not produced.
6. The statement under s.351 BNSS (old s.313 CrPC) did not put the incriminating circumstances of
   ___ to the accused.
7. The sentence is excessive; mitigating factors: ___.
PRAYER: Set aside the conviction and sentence and acquit the appellant. In the alternative, reduce
the sentence. [File an application under s.430 BNSS for suspension of sentence and bail.]
```

## 10. Criminal revision petition (s.438 read with s.442 BNSS, old ss.397 and 401 CrPC)

Lies against final or intermediate orders, not purely interlocutory ones (s.438(2)). Plead the
order, its illegality, impropriety or incorrectness, and the resulting miscarriage of justice.
Limitation 90 days (Art 131). A second revision by the same party is barred where one has already
been decided by the Sessions Court (s.438(3)) [VERIFY the sub-section numbers].

## 11. Other common applications

| Application | Provision (old provision) | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| Summons to produce a document or thing | s.94 BNSS (s.91 CrPC) | Identify the document, who holds it, and why it is necessary or desirable for the inquiry or trial |
| Recall or examination of a witness | s.348 BNSS (s.311 CrPC) | Why the evidence is essential to a just decision; not to fill gaps in a party's case |
| Summoning an additional accused | s.358 BNSS (s.319 CrPC) | Evidence at trial showing the person's involvement; the test is more than a prima facie case [VERIFY: *Hardeep Singh v. State of Punjab*, (2014) 3 SCC 92] |
| Exemption from personal appearance | s.228 BNSS (s.205 CrPC) at the summons stage; s.355 BNSS (s.317 CrPC) during trial [VERIFY] | Reason (health, distance, occupation), an undertaking not to dispute identity, and appearance through counsel |
| Interim custody of property (vehicle, jewellery, documents) | s.497 and s.503 BNSS (ss.451 and 457 CrPC) [VERIFY] | Ownership proof, an undertaking to produce when required, photographs and a panchanama, a bond. Courts favour early release of vehicles [VERIFY: *Sunderbhai Ambalal Desai v. State of Gujarat*, (2002) 10 SCC 283] |
| Compounding | s.359 BNSS (s.320 CrPC) | Joint memo signed by the parties, identified before the court; only offences listed as compoundable, some only with the court's permission |
| Copies of the police report and documents | s.230 BNSS (s.207 CrPC) | The accused's right to copies; list what is missing |
| Further investigation | s.193(9) BNSS (s.173(8) CrPC) | New material, or material ignored, that needs investigation |
