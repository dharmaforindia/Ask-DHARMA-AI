# Family law and special forums

Substantive law, forum and strategy for these areas are in the `practice-areas-india` skill. Items
marked [VERIFY] need checking against the current Act and rules.

## 1. Divorce petition (contested), Hindu Marriage Act, 1955, s.13

```
IN THE COURT OF THE ___ PRINCIPAL JUDGE, FAMILY COURT, AT ___
M.C. NO. ___ OF 20__

PETITIONER: [husband / wife, age, address]   RESPONDENT: [spouse]

PETITION UNDER SECTION 13(1)(___) OF THE HINDU MARRIAGE ACT, 1955 FOR A DECREE OF DIVORCE

1. The marriage was solemnised on ___ at ___ according to Hindu rites (photographs, invitation,
   marriage certificate: Annex __). Children: ___ (names, ages, who has custody).
2. Last resided together at ___ (jurisdiction under s.19).
3. Facts constituting [cruelty / desertion for 2 years / adultery / conversion / mental disorder /
   ...]: specific incidents with dates, places and witnesses. Avoid vague allegations.
4. The petition is not presented in collusion with the respondent; there is no unnecessary or
   improper delay; the petitioner has not condoned the conduct (s.23).
5. Earlier proceedings between the parties (maintenance, DV Act, s.85 BNS / s.498A IPC case, custody):
   ___.
PRAYER: Dissolve the marriage by a decree of divorce; [custody of the child; permanent alimony;
return of stridhan]; costs.
VERIFICATION.
```

Interim applications: maintenance pendente lite and litigation expenses (s.24 HMA); custody and
visitation (s.26 HMA). In maintenance matters, file the affidavit of assets, income and liabilities
in the format prescribed in *Rajnesh v. Neha*, (2021) 2 SCC 324 [VERIFY].

## 2. Divorce by mutual consent (s.13B HMA)

```
JOINT PETITION UNDER SECTION 13B OF THE HINDU MARRIAGE ACT, 1955

1. Particulars of the marriage and the children.
2. The parties have been living separately since ___ (one year or more) and have not been able to
   live together.
3. They have mutually agreed that the marriage be dissolved.
4. Terms of settlement (free consent, no coercion):
   a) permanent alimony of Rs. ___ in full and final settlement, paid as ___;
   b) custody of ___ with ___; visitation for the other parent: ___;
   c) return of articles / stridhan: ___;
   d) withdrawal of all pending cases between them (list them) within ___ days of the decree;
   e) no further claims against each other.
PRAYER: After the period prescribed by law, dissolve the marriage by a decree of divorce by mutual
consent. [If appropriate: an application to waive the six-month waiting period.]
```

The six-month period between the first and second motions can be waived in suitable cases
[VERIFY: *Amardeep Singh v. Harveen Kaur*, (2017) 8 SCC 746]. Keep the settlement terms precise and
enforceable, and time the payments to the second motion.

## 3. Restitution of conjugal rights (s.9 HMA)

Plead the marriage, that the respondent withdrew from the petitioner's society on ___ without
reasonable excuse, the petitioner's efforts to resume cohabitation, and readiness to live with the
respondent. Use only where the client genuinely wants to resume the marriage.

## 4. Maintenance (s.144 BNSS, old s.125 CrPC)

```
PETITION UNDER SECTION 144 OF THE BNSS FOR MAINTENANCE
1. Relationship: wife / child / parent of the respondent.
2. The petitioner is unable to maintain herself / himself: ___.
3. The respondent has sufficient means (income, assets, lifestyle with evidence) and has
   neglected or refused to maintain the petitioner since ___.
4. Monthly needs: food, rent, medical, education, other: Rs. ___.
PRAYER: Monthly maintenance of Rs. ___ from the date of the application; interim maintenance of
Rs. ___.
[File the affidavit of assets and liabilities.]
```

## 5. Domestic violence: application under s.12, Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005

Plead the domestic relationship and the shared household; specific incidents of physical, sexual,
verbal or emotional, or economic abuse with dates; the Domestic Incident Report, if any; and the
reliefs sought: protection order (s.18), residence order (s.19), monetary relief (s.20), custody
(s.21), compensation (s.22), and interim and ex parte orders (s.23). On the shared household, see
*Satish Chander Ahuja v. Sneha Ahuja*, (2021) 1 SCC 414 [VERIFY].

## 6. Custody and guardianship

Under the Guardians and Wards Act, 1890 (s.7 appointment, s.25 return of a ward) and the Hindu
Minority and Guardianship Act, 1956. The welfare of the child is the paramount consideration. Plead:
the child's age, schooling and health; the child's present situation; each parent's ability to care
for the child (home, time, support); the child's wishes if old enough; and proposed visitation for
the other parent.

## 7. Consumer complaint (Consumer Protection Act, 2019, s.35)

```
BEFORE THE ___ DISTRICT / STATE CONSUMER DISPUTES REDRESSAL COMMISSION, ___
CONSUMER COMPLAINT NO. ___ OF 20__

1. The complainant is a "consumer" (s.2(7)): bought goods / hired services for consideration of
   Rs. ___ on ___, not for a commercial purpose.
2. The opposite party: ___.
3. Deficiency in service / defect in goods / unfair trade practice: ___ (with documents).
4. Notice / complaints to the opposite party: ___.
5. Pecuniary jurisdiction (based on the consideration paid) and territorial jurisdiction (where
   the opposite party works or where the complainant resides or personally works, s.34(2)).
6. Limitation: within two years of the cause of action (s.69).
PRAYER (s.39): refund / replacement / repair / compensation of Rs. ___ for loss and mental agony /
costs / discontinuing the unfair trade practice.
[Affidavit; e-filing on the e-Daakhil portal where available [VERIFY].]
```

## 8. RERA complaint (Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016, s.31)

Before the Karnataka Real Estate Regulatory Authority (or its Adjudicating Officer for
compensation). Plead the agreement for sale and allotment, the RERA registration number of the
project, payments made (table with dates and amounts), the promised possession date and the delay,
and the relief: refund with interest (s.18(1)), or interest for every month of delay while staying
in the project (proviso to s.18(1)), or compensation (s.71 adjudication). Use the Authority's
prescribed form and fee [VERIFY].

---

## 9. Motor accident claim (Motor Vehicles Act, 1988, s.166)

Before the Motor Accidents Claims Tribunal. Plead: the accident (date, time, place, how it
happened, the vehicles and their registration numbers); the FIR and charge-sheet; the owner, driver
and insurer; for death, the deceased's age, occupation and income, and the dependants; for injury,
the injuries, treatment, expenses, disability and loss of earnings; and the compensation claimed
with a computation (multiplier, future prospects, deductions, conventional heads). File within six
months of the accident (s.166(3) as amended in 2019) [VERIFY].

## 10. Arbitration petitions (Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996)

| Petition | Forum | Plead |
|---|---|---|
| s.9 interim measures | Principal civil court of original jurisdiction (Commercial Court where applicable) | The arbitration agreement; the dispute; the s.21 notice (or the intention to start arbitration within 90 days of the order, s.9(2)); prima facie case, balance of convenience, irreparable harm; the specific measure (injunction, deposit, attachment, preservation) |
| s.11 appointment of an arbitrator | High Court (domestic) / Supreme Court (international commercial) | The agreement, the appointment procedure, the notice invoking arbitration and its failure. The court's inquiry is limited to whether an arbitration agreement exists [VERIFY the current position] |
| s.34 setting aside an award | The court under s.2(1)(e) | The award; receipt date; grounds under s.34(2) (incapacity, invalid agreement, no proper notice, beyond the scope of the reference, improper composition) and s.34(2A) (patent illegality, for domestic awards). Within 3 months of receiving the award, plus 30 days at most on sufficient cause (s.34(3)) |
| s.36(2) stay of the award | The same court | Grounds and an offer of security (the court may order a deposit for money awards) |

## 11. Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (summary)

- **Operational creditor:** send a demand notice in Form 3 or an invoice in Form 4 (s.8). If no
  payment is made and no pre-existing dispute is raised within 10 days, file an application under
  s.9 in Form 5 before the NCLT. A pre-existing genuine dispute defeats the application [VERIFY:
  *Mobilox Innovations v. Kirusa Software*, (2018) 1 SCC 353].
- **Financial creditor:** an application under s.7 in Form 1, with the record of default.
- Default threshold: Rs. 1 crore (notification of 24 March 2020) [VERIFY].
- The NCLT bench for Karnataka sits in Bengaluru.

## 12. SARFAESI Act: securitisation application before the DRT (s.17)

File within 45 days of the measure under s.13(4) (possession notice, sale notice). Plead the loan,
the s.13(2) notice and its date, the borrower's objection under s.13(3A) and the bank's reply,
the measure taken, and the illegality (no valid demand notice, objection not answered, the account
wrongly classified as an NPA, the sale notice gave less than 30 days, the reserve price was not
fixed properly, or the property is agricultural land). Writ petitions are not ordinarily entertained
against SARFAESI action because of this remedy [VERIFY: *United Bank of India v. Satyawati Tondon*,
(2010) 8 SCC 110].

## 13. Service matter before the Karnataka State Administrative Tribunal (KSAT)

Application under s.19 of the Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985. Plead the applicant's service
details; the impugned order; the representation or departmental appeal made and its result (s.20:
remedies under service rules must normally be exhausted); the grounds; and the relief. Limitation:
one year from the final order, or as provided in s.21 [VERIFY].

---

## 14. RTI application (s.6) and first appeal (s.19(1)), Right to Information Act, 2005

- **Application:** to the Public Information Officer of the department; list the information
  sought in numbered, specific items (documents, file notings, dates); pay the fee prescribed by the
  Karnataka RTI rules [VERIFY]; no reasons are needed.
- **Reply:** due in 30 days (48 hours where the life or liberty of a person is concerned) (s.7).
- **First appeal:** to the First Appellate Authority within 30 days of the reply or of the deadline
  (s.19(1)). **Second appeal:** to the Karnataka Information Commission within 90 days (s.19(3)).

## 15. Election petition (Representation of the People Act, 1951)

Filed in the High Court within 45 days of the date of election of the returned candidate (s.81).
Contents under s.83: a concise statement of material facts, full particulars of any corrupt
practice (names, dates, places), signed and verified like a plaint; where corrupt practice is
alleged, an affidavit in the prescribed form (Form 25) [VERIFY]. Defects in material facts are
fatal, so plead every material fact. Grounds: s.100. Corrupt practices: s.123.
