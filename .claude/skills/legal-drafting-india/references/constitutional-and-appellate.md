## 1. Public Interest Litigation (PIL)

Courts examine PILs closely for bona fides. A PIL that looks like a private or political grievance
is dismissed, often with costs. Check the Karnataka High Court's current PIL rules for the
mandatory disclosures [VERIFY the rules' name and year].

```
WRIT PETITION (PIL) NO. ___ OF 20__
UNDER ARTICLE 226 OF THE CONSTITUTION, IN PUBLIC INTEREST

1. The petitioner: credentials, public work, address, and source of income. The petitioner has no
   personal, private or oblique interest in this matter.
2. The public injury: who is affected (the class of persons), how many, and how. Why they cannot
   approach the court themselves.
3. Research done by the petitioner: sources, RTI replies, photographs, reports (annexed).
4. Representations made to the authorities on ___, and their result.
5. No other PIL on the same subject has been filed by the petitioner or, to the petitioner's
   knowledge, by anyone else [or: details].
6. The legal duty of the respondents: statute, rules, policy, constitutional provision.
7. GROUNDS.
PRAYER: specific, workable directions (for example, to act on a statutory duty within a time,
to file a compliance report, or to form a committee).
```

Guidelines on entertaining PILs: *State of Uttaranchal v. Balwant Singh Chaufal*, (2010) 3 SCC 402
[VERIFY].

## 2. Habeas corpus (WPHC)

```
WRIT PETITION (HABEAS CORPUS) NO. ___ OF 20__

PETITIONER: [the detenu, or a relative on the detenu's behalf]
RESPONDENTS: 1. State of Karnataka, by its Additional Chief Secretary, Home Department.
             2. The detaining authority (Commissioner of Police / District Magistrate).
             3. The Superintendent, ___ Central Prison.

1. The detention order dated ___ under section ___ of ___ Act (Annexure-A) and the grounds of
   detention served on ___ (Annexure-B).
2. GROUNDS (each a separate legal infirmity):
   a) The grounds and relied-on documents were not supplied in a language the detenu understands /
      were not supplied in time, violating Art 22(5).
   b) The detenu's representation dated ___ was not considered, or was considered after an
      unexplained delay.
   c) The detaining authority did not record its subjective satisfaction on relevant material;
      stale incidents were relied on; there is no live link between the incidents and the
      detention.
   d) Ordinary criminal law was enough to deal with the alleged conduct: the detenu was already in
      custody / on bail, and the authority did not consider this.
   e) The approval or confirmation by the State Government / Advisory Board was not within the
      statutory time.
PRAYER: A writ of habeas corpus quashing the detention order dated ___ and directing the
respondents to release the detenu forthwith.
```

Note: Preventive detention is construed strictly. Every procedural safeguard counts, so build the
case from the dates. Get the full detention file in the respondents' objections.

## 3. Writ appeal memorandum (s.4 Karnataka High Court Act, 1961)

```
WRIT APPEAL NO. ___ OF 20__
IN WRIT PETITION NO. ___ OF 20__

MEMORANDUM OF WRIT APPEAL UNDER SECTION 4 OF THE KARNATAKA HIGH COURT ACT, 1961 AGAINST THE ORDER
DATED ___ PASSED BY THE LEARNED SINGLE JUDGE IN W.P. NO. ___

GROUNDS
1. The learned Single Judge erred in holding ___.
2. The learned Single Judge failed to consider ___ (Annexure-__ in the writ petition).
3. The order is contrary to ___ (binding authority, para __).
PRAYER: Set aside the order dated ___ and allow the writ petition as prayed.
```

Limitation: 30 days (Limitation Act, Art 117). Check first that the single judge exercised original
jurisdiction under Art 226 and not supervisory jurisdiction under Art 227.

## 4. Civil contempt petition (CCC)

```
CIVIL CONTEMPT PETITION NO. ___ OF 20__
UNDER SECTIONS 11 AND 12 OF THE CONTEMPT OF COURTS ACT, 1971

1. The order dated ___ in ___ directed the respondent(s) to ___ within ___ (Annexure-A).
2. The order was served on / communicated to the respondent(s) on ___ (Annexure-B).
3. The time for compliance ended on ___. Despite a reminder dated ___ (Annexure-C), the respondent(s)
   have not complied. The disobedience is wilful because ___.
4. The accused / contemnors: [name each officer by name and designation, being the persons
   responsible for compliance].
5. This petition is filed within one year of the default (s.20).
PRAYER: Initiate contempt proceedings against the respondent(s), punish them under s.12, and
direct compliance with the order dated ___.
```

Note: Contempt is for wilful disobedience of a clear order. It is not a way to get a fresh
direction on a point the order did not decide.

## 5. Transfer petitions in the Supreme Court

| Type | Provision | Plead |
|---|---|---|
| Civil (including matrimonial) | s.25 CPC | The pending case; hardship (distance, a young child, no income, safety, health); that the respondent is better placed to travel. Offer to pay travel costs or accept video hearings where it helps |
| Criminal | s.446 BNSS (old s.406 CrPC) | A reasonable apprehension that a fair trial is not possible, with specific facts; convenience is not enough on its own |
| Cases raising the same question of law | Art 139A | The cases pending in different High Courts and the common question |

## 6. Review petition in the Supreme Court (Art 137; SC Rules Order XLVII)

```
REVIEW PETITION (CIVIL / CRIMINAL) NO. ___ OF 20__
IN [SLP / CIVIL APPEAL / WRIT PETITION] NO. ___ OF 20__

1. The judgment / order dated ___ under review.
2. GROUNDS: an error apparent on the face of the record, namely ___ [or the other grounds in
   Order XLVII Rule 1 CPC for civil matters]. No new argument is raised.
3. Certificate: this review petition is filed within 30 days and raises only the grounds stated.
PRAYER: Review the judgment / order dated ___ [and list the petition for an oral hearing in open
court].
```

## 7. Curative petition (*Rupa Ashok Hurra*; SC Rules Order XLVIII)

Only after the review petition is dismissed. Grounds are limited to a breach of natural justice
(for example, the petitioner was not served or not heard although affected) or the judge's failure
to disclose a connection giving rise to an apprehension of bias. It must state that these grounds
were taken in the review petition and dismissed by circulation, and it must carry a **certificate by
a Senior Advocate** that it meets these requirements [VERIFY the current rule text].

## 8. Writ petition under Art 32 (Supreme Court)

Use the Art 226 skeleton in `core-litigation.md`, with these changes: file in the Supreme Court
through an Advocate-on-Record; identify the fundamental right breached (Arts 14, 19, 21, 22 and so
on) at the start; and explain why the petitioner has come directly to the Supreme Court and not the
High Court (for example, an issue affecting several States, or an emergency). Expect to be asked
to go to the High Court unless there is a good reason.

## 9. SLP (criminal): differences from the civil SLP

- Heading: "CRIMINAL APPELLATE JURISDICTION, SPECIAL LEAVE PETITION (CRIMINAL) NO. ___".
- A petitioner sentenced to imprisonment must file proof of surrender, or an application for
  exemption from surrendering with reasons.
- Give the custody period in a table (date of arrest, periods on bail, total custody) and the
  details of the sentence.
- For bail SLPs: state the allegations, the stage of the trial, the custody period, the bail orders
  of the co-accused (parity) and any antecedents.

## 10. Interlocutory applications in the Supreme Court

| Application | Plead |
|---|---|
| Stay / interim relief | Prima facie case, balance of convenience, irreparable harm; offer conditions (deposit, undertaking) |
| Exemption from filing the certified copy | The copy was applied for on ___ and is awaited; a true copy is filed; undertake to file it |
| Condonation of delay | An explanation for every period of delay with dates |
| Permission to file additional documents | The documents, why they were not filed earlier, and their relevance |
| Exemption from surrendering | Reasons (age, health, the short sentence already undergone) |
| Impleadment or intervention | The applicant's direct interest and how the outcome affects them |
| Early listing / urgent hearing | The specific urgency with dates |
| Permission to file a lengthy list of dates | Why the record needs it |

## 11. Counter-affidavit and rejoinder (Supreme Court and High Court)

**Counter-affidavit (respondent):** preliminary submissions (maintainability, suppression, delay,
the scope of Art 136 or Art 226), a brief statement of the true facts, a para-wise reply, and
supporting annexures (R-1, R-2 ...). Do not deny what the record shows.

**Rejoinder (petitioner):** answer only new facts and new documents raised in the counter. Do not
repeat the petition. Point out admissions in the counter.
