# Case lifecycle: from the first meeting to the final order

Section numbers are given for the new criminal laws first, with the old ones in brackets. Check
every number against the bare Act before filing. Limitation periods below are the usual ones under
the Limitation Act, 1963 or the named statute. Special statutes can change them.

## Stage 1. Intake

Ask these questions at the first meeting:

- Who is the client, and what is their role (petitioner, accused, complainant, plaintiff,
  defendant, respondent)? Who are all the opposite parties, including government departments and
  officers? Run a conflict check.
- What happened, in date order? What does the client actually want: liberty, money, possession, a
  stay, a quick end, or to protect their name?
- What has already happened legally? FIR number and police station, crime number, case number and
  court, the last order, the next date of hearing.
- Which documents exist? Who holds the originals? Which are in Kannada or another language and will
  need translation?
- What is the deadline? The date of the impugned order and the date it was received; any risk of
  arrest, demolition, auction, dispossession or a lapse of rights.
- Has there been any earlier litigation on the same subject? It must be disclosed in the petition.
- What is the budget, how much delay and risk will the client accept, and are they open to
  settlement?

Engagement:

- Fee terms in writing. No fee that depends on the result (BCI Rules, Part VI, Ch II, Rule 20).
- Vakalatnama signed and accepted.
- Confidentiality. Professional communications are privileged under s.132 BSA (old s.126 Evidence
  Act) [VERIFY the section number].
- Tell the client in writing what you need from them and by when.

## Stage 2. Facts

Build the chronology as a table:

| # | Date | Event | Source document | Page | Proved, provable or alleged | Comment |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Then:

- Mark the facts that are **decisive**: those the result turns on.
- Mark the **gaps**: facts the other side will exploit, and documents still to be found (RTI
  requests, certified copies, the revenue record, bank statements, call records, CCTV).
- Electronic evidence needs a certificate under s.63 BSA (old s.65B Evidence Act). Arrange it early.
  *Arjun Panditrao Khotkar v. Kailash Kushanrao Gorantyal*, (2020) 7 SCC 1, holds that the
  certificate is mandatory [VERIFY].

## Stage 3. Gatekeeping checklist

Do this before drafting anything.

**Limitation**
- [ ] Find the correct Article of the Limitation Act or the period in the special statute (table
      below).
- [ ] Exclude the time taken to obtain a certified copy (s.12) and time spent in good faith before a
      wrong forum (s.14). Check for an acknowledgment of liability (s.18) or part payment (s.19).
- [ ] If late, prepare a condonation application under s.5 with an explanation for the whole period
      of delay. Section 5 does not apply to suits.
- [ ] Writs have no fixed limitation, but delay and laches defeat them. Explain any delay in the
      petition itself.

**Jurisdiction**
- [ ] Civil: ss.15 to 20 CPC (pecuniary value, where the property is, where the defendant lives,
      where the cause of action arose).
- [ ] Criminal: the place of inquiry and trial (Chapter XIV BNSS) [VERIFY the sections].
- [ ] Writ: Art 226(2) allows a petition where any part of the cause of action arose.
- [ ] Commercial: check that the dispute is a "commercial dispute" under s.2(1)(c) and meets the
      "specified value" under the Commercial Courts Act, 2015.

**Maintainability and bars**
- [ ] Alternative remedy. A writ still lies to enforce a fundamental right, for a breach of natural
      justice, where the order is without jurisdiction, or where the vires of a law is challenged.
      *M/s Radha Krishan Industries v. State of Himachal Pradesh*, [2021] 3 SCR 406, sets out the
      principles.
- [ ] Whether the respondent is "State" or performs a public function (Art 12).
- [ ] Res judicata (s.11 CPC), res sub judice (s.10), Order II Rule 2, and implied bars under s.9.
- [ ] An arbitration clause (s.8 Arbitration Act).
- [ ] A special statute that bars the civil court (for example s.34 SARFAESI Act, or the Debts
      Recovery Tribunal's exclusive jurisdiction).
- [ ] A writ petition withdrawn earlier without liberty bars a fresh one on the same cause:
      *Sarguja Transport Service v. State Transport Appellate Tribunal*, (1987) 1 SCC 5 [VERIFY].

**Parties**
- [ ] All necessary and proper parties (Order I Rules 9 and 10 CPC).
- [ ] The State is sued through the proper officer (Art 300; s.79 CPC). In Karnataka: "State of
      Karnataka, represented by its Principal Secretary / Secretary, Department of ___".

**Notices before suing**
- [ ] s.80 CPC: two months' notice before suing the Government or a public officer. Leave to sue
      without notice is available for urgent relief (s.80(2)).
- [ ] s.12A Commercial Courts Act: pre-institution mediation is mandatory unless urgent interim
      relief is sought. *M/s Patil Automation Pvt. Ltd. v. Rakheja Engineers Pvt. Ltd.*, [2022] 11
      SCR 808 (17 August 2022).
- [ ] s.138 NI Act: a demand notice within 30 days of being told of the dishonour (table below).
- [ ] Any statutory appeal or representation that must be exhausted first.

## Stage 4. Issues and law

- Write each issue as a question that can be answered yes or no, in the client's favour if possible.
- For each issue, find the governing provision, the leading Supreme Court authority, the latest
  Supreme Court authority, the Karnataka High Court authority, and any authority against you.
- Record each authority's verification status.

## Stage 5. Case theory

- **Story:** one paragraph explaining why the client should win, in plain language.
- **Theme:** one sentence a judge will remember. For example: "The authority decided first and gave
  the hearing afterwards."
- **Three best points**, in order of strength.
- **The opponent's three best points**, each with your answer.
- **The concession you will make** to protect your credibility.

## Stage 6. Strategy

Options to compare by goal:

| Goal | Options |
|---|---|
| Stop something now | Interim injunction (Order XXXIX Rules 1 and 2 CPC), writ with interim stay, s.9 Arbitration Act, caveat to prevent ex parte orders, anticipatory bail |
| Get money | Suit (summary suit under Order XXXVII where it applies), s.138 NI Act complaint, arbitration, IBC application (threshold and default), DRT |
| Get or keep property | Suit for declaration and possession, injunction, s.6 Specific Relief Act (possession within 6 months of dispossession), revenue proceedings |
| Liberty | Anticipatory bail, regular bail, default bail, quashing, a challenge to detention by habeas corpus |
| Clear the client's name | Discharge (s.250 / s.262 BNSS), quashing, an acquittal appeal strategy |
| Settle | Mediation (s.89 CPC), Lok Adalat, compounding (s.359 BNSS), compromise-based quashing |

For each option, record the remedy, forum, cost, time, chance of interim relief, risks, and the
effect on other proceedings (for example, whether admissions in one case will be used in another).

## Stage 7. Tracks by type of matter

### A. Civil suit (trial court to High Court to Supreme Court)

1. Legal notice (s.80 CPC where needed), then plaint (Order VII) with the correct valuation and court
   fee under the Karnataka Court-fees and Suits Valuation Act, 1958.
2. Interim application under Order XXXIX Rules 1 and 2: prima facie case, balance of convenience,
   irreparable injury (*Dalpat Kumar v. Prahlad Singh*, (1992) 1 SCC 719 [VERIFY]). For an ex parte
   order, record the reasons under Order XXXIX Rule 3.
3. Defendant: written statement within 30 days, extendable to 90 (Order VIII Rule 1). In commercial
   suits, 120 days is the outer limit and cannot be extended (*SCG Contracts India Pvt. Ltd. v. K.S.
   Chamankar Infrastructure Pvt. Ltd.*, (2019) 12 SCC 210 [VERIFY]). Consider rejection of the plaint
   (Order VII Rule 11) on the plaint's own averments.
4. Admission and denial of documents, framing of issues (Order XIV), examination-in-chief by
   affidavit (Order XVIII Rule 4), cross-examination.
5. Object to the mode of proof of a document when it is marked, or the objection is lost [VERIFY:
   *R.V.E. Venkatachala Gounder v. Arulmigu Viswesaraswami*, (2003) 8 SCC 752].
6. Arguments with written submissions, judgment, decree.
7. Execution (Order XXI). First appeal (s.96 and Order XLI). Second appeal on a substantial
   question of law only (s.100). Then an SLP.

### B. Criminal: for the accused

1. Before arrest: anticipatory bail (s.482 BNSS, old s.438 CrPC). Insist on a notice under s.35(3)
   BNSS (old s.41A CrPC) where the offence is punishable with up to 7 years (*Arnesh Kumar v. State
   of Bihar*, [2014] 8 SCR 128).
2. Quashing if the FIR shows no offence or is an abuse of process (s.528 BNSS, old s.482 CrPC). The
   High Court should not casually grant "no coercive steps" orders while declining to quash
   (*Neeharika Infrastructure Pvt. Ltd. v. State of Maharashtra*, [2021] 4 SCR 1044).
3. After arrest: watch the remand under s.187 BNSS (old s.167 CrPC). Apply for default bail as soon
   as 60 or 90 days pass without a charge-sheet. Apply for regular bail (s.480 or s.483 BNSS).
4. Charge-sheet (s.193 BNSS, old s.173 CrPC). Obtain copies of the documents. Apply for discharge
   (s.250 BNSS for Sessions cases, s.262 BNSS for warrant cases before a Magistrate).
5. Charge (s.251 BNSS). Trial: plan the cross-examination of each prosecution witness. Use earlier
   police statements only to contradict (s.181 BNSS, old s.162 CrPC).
6. Statement of the accused (s.351 BNSS, old s.313 CrPC). Defence evidence. Arguments.
7. If convicted: sentence hearing, appeal (s.415 BNSS, old s.374 CrPC), suspension of sentence and
   bail pending appeal (s.430 BNSS, old s.389 CrPC). Then revision or SLP.

### C. Criminal: for the complainant or victim

1. If the police refuse to register an FIR for a cognizable offence: approach the Superintendent of
   Police, then the Magistrate under s.175(3) BNSS (old s.156(3) CrPC). Registration is mandatory
   for cognizable offences, subject to the preliminary-inquiry categories in *Lalita Kumari v. Govt.
   of U.P.*, [2013] 14 SCR 713. Check BNSS s.173(3) on preliminary inquiry for offences punishable
   with 3 to 7 years.
2. Private complaint (s.223 BNSS, old s.200 CrPC).
3. Oppose bail with specific facts (risk of absconding, tampering with witnesses, antecedents). Seek
   cancellation of bail on supervening misconduct.
4. Victim's right to appeal (proviso to s.413 BNSS, old proviso to s.372 CrPC) [VERIFY].

### D. Writ: Art 226, then writ appeal, then SLP

1. Give a representation to the authority first and wait a reasonable time, unless it is urgent.
2. Draft the writ petition: facts, the impugned action, grounds, the case for interim relief and the
   prayer. Annex the documents (Annexure-A, B, C ...). Disclose all earlier proceedings.
3. Serve an advance copy on the government counsel's office.
4. Hearing on admission: "rule nisi" or notice; interim order.
5. The respondent files a statement of objections. File a rejoinder only to answer new facts.
6. Final hearing, then a writ appeal under s.4 Karnataka High Court Act (30 days), then an SLP.

### E. Commercial disputes and arbitration

1. Pre-institution mediation (s.12A CCA) unless urgent interim relief is needed.
2. Arbitration: notice invoking arbitration (s.21), interim measures (s.9 before the tribunal is
   constituted, s.17 after), appointment application (s.11), challenge to jurisdiction (s.16).
3. An unstamped arbitration agreement is not void or unenforceable at the referral stage. Stamping
   is decided by the tribunal (*In re Interplay between Arbitration Agreements under the A&C Act,
   1996 and the Indian Stamp Act, 1899*, [2023] 15 SCR 1081 : 2023 INSC 1066, Seven-Judge Bench).
4. Challenge to an award (s.34, on the limited grounds only). Courts have a limited power to modify
   an award (*Gayatri Balasamy v. M/s ISG Novasoft Technologies Ltd.*, [2025] 4 SCR 2080 : 2025 INSC
   605, Constitution Bench, 30 April 2025). Read the judgment for the exact limits.
5. Enforcement (s.36). Appeal (s.37).

## Stage 10. After the order

1. Apply for the certified copy the same day. The time taken is excluded from limitation (s.12).
2. Read the operative part carefully: what was granted, the time for compliance, liberty given, and
   costs.
3. Report to the client in writing: the result, what it means, the next deadline, and the options.
4. Compliance: serve the order on the authority with a covering letter and keep proof of service.
   If it is not obeyed, send a reminder, then file a contempt petition (in Karnataka, a CCC) within
   the year.
5. Execution of a decree under Order XXI CPC.
6. Decide on an appeal, review or SLP well within the limitation period. Calendar the last date and
   a target date 15 days earlier.
7. Close the file properly: return the originals, send the final bill, and record the outcome.
