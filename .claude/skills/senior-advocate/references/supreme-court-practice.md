# Supreme Court of India: practice notes

The Registry's checklists, formats and listing practice change often. Check the current Supreme
Court Rules, 2013, the practice directions and the circulars on the Supreme Court website before
filing. Items marked [VERIFY] are from memory and need checking.

## Who does what

- **Advocate-on-Record (AOR)** (SC Rules, 2013, Order IV). Only an AOR can file a matter and act for
  a party in the Supreme Court. Any advocate may argue when instructed by the AOR.
- **Senior Advocate** (s.16 Advocates Act). Cannot file a vakalatnama, appears only with an AOR or
  another advocate, and does not take instructions directly from the client. A Senior Advocate may
  settle drafts but does not draw pleadings (BCI Rules, Part VI, Chapter I) [VERIFY].
- This skill helps with both kinds of work: the AOR's drafting and filing, and the arguing counsel's
  preparation.

## Jurisdictions and remedies

| Remedy | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Special Leave Petition (civil or criminal) | Art 136; SC Rules Order XXI (civil) and Order XXII (criminal) | Discretionary. The Court interferes only for special reasons such as grave injustice or a substantial question of law (*Pritam Singh v. State*, AIR 1950 SC 169 [VERIFY]) |
| Writ petition | Art 32; SC Rules Order XXXVIII [VERIFY] | Only for enforcing fundamental rights. The Court often sends petitioners to the High Court under Art 226 |
| Statutory appeals | The particular Act | For example s.62 IBC, s.67 Consumer Protection Act 2019, s.22 NGT Act, s.125 Electricity Act, s.53T Competition Act, s.15Z SEBI Act. Check each Act for its own limitation period |
| Criminal appeal as of right | Art 134; Supreme Court (Enlargement of Criminal Appellate Jurisdiction) Act, 1970 | Where the High Court reversed an acquittal and imposed death, life imprisonment or at least 10 years [VERIFY] |
| Transfer petition | s.25 CPC (civil); s.446 BNSS, old s.406 CrPC (criminal); Art 139A | Matrimonial transfers are common. Show real hardship, not just convenience |
| Review | Art 137; SC Rules Order XLVII | 30 days. Usually decided in chambers by circulation. Only for an error apparent on the face of the record or the other grounds in Order XLVII Rule 1 CPC. It is not a rehearing (*Kamlesh Verma v. Mayawati*, (2013) 8 SCC 320 [VERIFY]) |
| Curative petition | *Rupa Ashok Hurra v. Ashok Hurra*, (2002) 4 SCC 388 [VERIFY]; SC Rules Order XLVIII | Only after a review is dismissed. Grounds: breach of natural justice (for example, the party was not heard) or bias. Must be certified by a Senior Advocate |
| Complete justice | Art 142 | Not a ground on its own. Use it to shape relief |
| Contempt | Art 129; Contempt of Courts Act, 1971 | |

**Merger.** When an SLP is dismissed at the start by a non-speaking order, the High Court's order
does not merge into it, and a review can still be filed in the High Court (*Kunhayammed v. State of
Kerala*, decided 19 July 2000, three judges, reported as (2000) 6 SCC 359 [VERIFY the SCC
citation]). Once leave is granted and the appeal is decided, the High Court's order merges.

**Withdrawing.** Do not withdraw without liberty. If the bench is against you, ask to withdraw with
liberty to take the other remedy (for example, a review in the High Court, or a representation).
Withdrawal without liberty can bar a second attempt.

## Anatomy of an SLP paper book

The usual order is below. Follow the Registry's current checklist exactly.

1. Listing proforma and the office report on limitation.
2. **Synopsis and List of Dates.** This is the most important part (see below).
3. The impugned judgment or order, as a certified copy. If it is not yet available, file an
   application for exemption from filing the certified copy.
4. The **Special Leave Petition**:
   - Questions of law.
   - Declaration that no other petition has been filed against the same order.
   - Declaration that the annexures were part of the record before the court below.
   - Grounds (A, B, C ...).
   - Grounds for interim relief.
   - Main prayer and prayer for interim relief.
5. Affidavit in support.
6. **Annexures (P-1, P-2 ...).** Only documents that were before the court below. A new document
   needs an application for permission to file additional documents.
7. Applications: condonation of delay (if late), exemption from filing the certified copy, stay or
   interim relief. In criminal SLPs by a convict sentenced to imprisonment: proof of surrender, or
   an application for exemption from surrendering.
8. Vakalatnama and memo of appearance by the AOR.

If the other side has lodged a caveat, serve the caveator before filing and state that service has
been made.

## Writing an SLP that gets notice issued

At the admission stage the bench reads the synopsis and the impugned order, often in a few minutes.

- **Synopsis (one to three pages).** Say who the parties are, what the High Court did, why that is
  wrong in law, and why the question matters beyond these parties. Put the strongest point in the
  first paragraph.
- **List of Dates.** Only the dates the case depends on, each with the annexure number.
- **Questions of law.** Five to eight at most. Each should be precise and answerable in the
  petitioner's favour. For example: "Whether the High Court, in second appeal, could reverse
  concurrent findings of fact without framing a substantial question of law under s.100 CPC?"
- **Grounds.** One point per ground. Lead with the best one. Cite the authority and the paragraph.
- **Why it is "special".** Show perversity (a finding no reasonable person could reach on the
  evidence), a failure to follow binding precedent, an error of jurisdiction, a conflict between High
  Courts, a question of public importance, or grave injustice. A simple error of fact is not enough.

## The admission hearing: the two-minute pitch

Assume you will get about two minutes. Say:

1. "My Lords, this is a special leave petition against the judgment of the High Court of Karnataka
   dated ___, which [holds / directs] ___."
2. "The short point is ___." (One sentence.)
3. "This is contrary to [binding authority], paragraph ___, which holds ___." Or: "The finding at
   paragraph ___ of the impugned judgment ignores the document at page ___."
4. "The consequence is ___." (Irreparable harm: eviction, demolition, custody, a large sum.)
5. "We pray for notice and, meanwhile, a stay of ___." Have a narrower interim prayer ready, such as
   status quo or no coercive steps.

Possible outcomes: dismissed; notice issued (with or without an interim order, sometimes limited
to one question); leave granted (it becomes a civil or criminal appeal); tagged with another matter;
disposed of with directions; or withdrawn with liberty.

Fresh matters are usually listed on miscellaneous days and regular hearings on other days. Check
the current cause list practice.

## Mentioning and urgent listing

Urgent matters such as arrest, demolition, eviction, a death sentence or habeas corpus are mentioned
with a mentioning memo stating the reason for urgency. The procedure (oral mentioning, email
mentioning, which bench) is set by the current Chief Justice's directions. Check the latest notice
before mentioning.

## Final hearing

- Written submissions, often with a page limit set by the bench.
- A compilation of judgments with the relevant paragraphs flagged, and a convenience compilation of
  the record.
- Follow any time limits for oral argument that the bench sets.
- Structure: the short point, the facts in brief with page numbers, propositions with authorities,
  answers to the other side, and the relief.

## Common reasons SLPs are dismissed, and how to avoid them

| Reason | How to avoid it |
|---|---|
| Concurrent findings of fact, with no perversity shown | Show the specific evidence ignored or misread, with page numbers |
| Suppression or misstatement of facts | Disclose everything, including earlier proceedings and adverse orders |
| Unexplained delay | Explain each period of delay with dates and documents |
| Challenge to an interlocutory order | Show irreparable harm, or wait for the final order |
| A remedy in the High Court not yet used (review, writ appeal) | From a Karnataka single judge's writ order, first file a writ appeal under s.4 Karnataka High Court Act |
| New points or new facts not raised below | Keep to pure questions of law that arise on the record |
| Annexures not before the court below | File an application for additional documents with reasons |
