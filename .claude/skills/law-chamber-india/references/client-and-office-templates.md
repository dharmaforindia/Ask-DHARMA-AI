## 2. Conflict check

Search the chamber's records for: the client; each opposite party; their directors, partners,
spouses and close relatives where relevant; and related entities. A conflict exists if the chamber
acts or has acted for the opposite party in the same or a connected matter, or holds confidential
information of the opposite party relevant to this matter. If there is a conflict, decline in
writing without disclosing the other client's confidences.

## 3. Engagement and fee letter

```
[Letterhead]                                                     Date: ___
To: [client]
Sub: Engagement for ___

1. Scope: We will act for you in ___ [court / forum, case no.]. This covers ___ [drafting, filing,
   appearances up to the stage of ___]. It does not cover appeals, execution or connected matters,
   which will need a separate engagement.
2. Fees: [a fixed fee of Rs. ___ for ___ / Rs. ___ per effective hearing and Rs. ___ per
   non-effective hearing / Rs. ___ for drafting]. Court fees, stamp duty, typing, translation,
   travel, certified copies and the fees of senior counsel, if engaged with your consent, are extra.
   [Bar Council rules do not permit a fee that depends on the result of the case or a share of
   what is recovered.]
3. Payment: ___ in advance; the balance ___.
4. Your responsibilities: give complete and true information and documents, tell us of any change,
   attend when required, and pay fees on time.
5. Our responsibilities: to act with diligence and keep you informed after each hearing. We cannot
   guarantee any result.
6. Communication: by ___ (email / WhatsApp to ___). Instructions on important steps (settlement,
   withdrawal, concessions) must be in writing.
7. Confidentiality: everything you tell us is confidential and privileged.
8. Ending the engagement: you may end it at any time. We may withdraw for sufficient cause with
   reasonable notice, and will hand over the papers on settlement of fees due.
Please sign and return a copy to confirm.
[Advocate's name, enrolment no.]                                  Accepted: [client]
```

## 7. Case diary and hearing log

| Date | Court / hall / item no. | Bench | Stage | What happened | Next date | Next step | Who | Done |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Write the entry the same day. Note any concession made, any undertaking given, and any direction
the court gave with its deadline.

## 8. Limitation and hearing calendar

For every deadline record: the matter, the event (order received, notice served), the governing
provision, the period, the date the period starts, excluded time, **the last date**, the internal
target date (15 days earlier), the reminder dates, and the person responsible. Re-check every
computed date with a second person. Watch court vacations: filing on the reopening day is allowed
only where the law permits (s.4 Limitation Act; s.10 General Clauses Act).

## 9. Brief and file structure

```
File: [client] v. [opposite party] — [case no.]
01 Correspondence and instructions
02 Engagement letter, fee record
03 Pleadings (ours and theirs), in date order
04 Orders and order sheets
05 Evidence: documents (exhibits list), depositions, witness statements
06 Research: memos, authorities, compilation
07 Hearing kits and notes of arguments
08 Drafts (dated versions)
09 Originals register (what we hold, where, received from)
```

Keep a one-page **brief summary** at the top of every file: parties, case number, court, stage,
next date, the client's goal, the core point, the key documents and the limitation dates.

## 10. Instructions to a junior or clerk

```
TO: ___   MATTER: ___   DUE: ___
TASK: ___ (one clear deliverable)
BACKGROUND: ___ (enough context; where the file is)
WHAT GOOD LOOKS LIKE: ___ (format, length, authorities to check)
DEADLINES: draft by ___; final by ___
QUESTIONS: ask me by ___ if anything is unclear
```

For clerks: filing lists (what to file, where, fee, copies), cause-list watch, certified copy
applications and collection, service and proof of service, and returning papers.

## 11. Court-day checklist

- The cause list checked the night before and the morning of the hearing: court hall, item number,
  bench.
- The brief: the paper book, the hearing kit, the compilation (copies for the bench and the other
  side), the original documents if needed, and the vakalatnama.
- The client informed of the hearing and whether they must attend.
- After the hearing: the diary entry, the client update, the next-date entry, and the application
  for a certified copy of any order.

**Virtual hearings:** a stable connection and a backup device, a quiet room with a plain background,
professional dress (bands), the camera at eye level, muted when not speaking, the documents open
and bookmarked, the link and meeting ID ready, and joining early. Follow the court's video
conferencing rules.

## 12. E-filing and e-courts tools

- **Supreme Court:** e-filing portal; the Advocate-on-Record files.
- **Karnataka High Court:** e-filing; the case status and order downloads; the cause list.
- **District courts:** the eCourts services portal and app (case status, orders, cause lists); the
  National Judicial Data Grid.
- Keep scanned copies in the formats and sizes the portal requires (searchable PDFs, bookmarks for
  annexures where required) [VERIFY current technical requirements].

## 13. Legal aid and Lok Adalat

- **Legal aid:** persons eligible under s.12 of the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987 (women,
  children, SC/ST members, persons in custody, persons below the income limit, and others) can get
  free legal services through the District Legal Services Authority, the Karnataka State Legal
  Services Authority or the High Court Legal Services Committee.
- **Lok Adalat:** an award is deemed a decree of a civil court, final and not appealable (s.21).
  Court fees paid are refunded when a pending case is settled there [VERIFY the refund rules].
  Useful for motor accident claims, cheque cases, matrimonial disputes, bank recoveries and
  compoundable offences.
