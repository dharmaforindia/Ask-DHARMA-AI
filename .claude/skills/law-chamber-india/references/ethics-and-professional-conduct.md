# Ethics and professional conduct

Sources: the Advocates Act, 1961 and the Bar Council of India Rules, Part VI, Chapter II
(Standards of Professional Conduct and Etiquette). Rule numbers marked [VERIFY] should be checked
against the current Rules before being quoted.

## 1. Duty to the court

- Act with dignity and self-respect; be respectful to the court without being servile.
- **Never mislead the court** on facts or law. Disclose binding authority against your client.
- Do not try to influence a judge by any means other than argument in court. No private
  communication with a judge about a pending case.
- Do not appear before a court where a close relative (as listed in the Rules) is a member of the
  bench [VERIFY the rule and the list of relatives].
- Do not act or plead in a matter in which you have a financial interest.
- Do not stand surety for a client.
- Wear the prescribed dress in court.
- Refuse to act in an illegal or improper manner even on the client's instructions.

## 2. Duty to the client

- **Fearlessly uphold the client's interests** by all fair and honourable means, regardless of
  personal consequences [VERIFY: Rule 15].
- Accept a brief in a court where you practise at your usual fee unless there are special
  circumstances; do not refuse without good reason.
- **Do not withdraw** from a case once accepted without sufficient cause and reasonable notice to the
  client. On withdrawal, refund any part of the fee not earned.
- **Confidentiality and privilege:** do not disclose client communications (s.132 BSA, old s.126
  Evidence Act), except as the law allows.
- **No contingent fees:** do not stipulate a fee contingent on the result or a share of the proceeds
  [VERIFY: Rule 20].
- Do not buy or bid for property sold in a proceeding in which you were engaged.
- Keep accounts of the client's money, and do not use it for your own purposes. Account for it when
  asked.
- Do not act for both sides in the same or a connected matter.
- Do not accept a brief where you are likely to be a witness.

## 3. Duty to the opponent and colleagues

- Do not negotiate directly with a party represented by an advocate, except through that advocate.
- Carry out legitimate promises made to the other side.
- Do not appear in a matter where another advocate has filed a vakalatnama for the same party
  without that advocate's consent (a no-objection), except in the circumstances the Rules allow.
- Be courteous to opponents in and out of court.

## 4. Advertising and solicitation

- **No soliciting work or advertising**, directly or indirectly, through circulars, touts, personal
  communications or interviews not warranted by personal relations, or by procuring newspaper
  comments or photographs about cases [VERIFY: Rule 36].
- An advocate may maintain a **website** with limited particulars as permitted by the BCI (name,
  address, contact details, enrolment number, the date of enrolment, professional and academic
  qualifications, and areas of practice) [VERIFY the current BCI resolution].
- Sign boards and name plates must be of reasonable size and not advertise.
- Social media posts about a client's case can amount to solicitation or breach confidentiality.
  Avoid them.

## 5. Misconduct and consequences

- Professional or other misconduct is punishable by the State Bar Council under s.35 of the
  Advocates Act (reprimand, suspension, removal from the roll), with appeals to the BCI (s.37) and the
  Supreme Court (s.38).
- **Common complaints:** neglect of the case (missed dates, failure to file), misappropriating client
  money, false statements to the court, misbehaviour with judges, and filing fabricated documents.
- **Fabricated or unverified AI citations** filed in court can lead to costs, adverse remarks and
  disciplinary action. Verify every authority yourself.

## 6. Senior Advocates

- Designated under s.16 of the Advocates Act. They do not file vakalatnamas, do not appear without an
  Advocate-on-Record or another advocate, do not accept instructions directly from clients, and do
  not draft pleadings (though they may settle them) [VERIFY the Rules in Part VI, Chapter I].
- Designation is by the Supreme Court and High Courts under their designation guidelines.

## 7. Practical ethics checklist

- [ ] Conflict checked before any advice.
- [ ] Fee terms in writing; no contingency element.
- [ ] Client told honestly of prospects and risks, in writing.
- [ ] Client instructions in writing for any settlement, withdrawal or concession.
- [ ] Nothing filed that the client has not approved and cannot honestly verify.
- [ ] All authorities verified; adverse binding authority disclosed.
- [ ] Victims' identities protected.
- [ ] Client money accounted for.
- [ ] Papers returned promptly when the engagement ends.
