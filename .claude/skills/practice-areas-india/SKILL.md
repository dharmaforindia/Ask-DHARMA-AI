# Practice-area playbooks (India)

> These playbooks give the map of each area: where to go, what governs it, the deadlines, the
> leading cases and the usual winning and losing points. They are not a substitute for reading the
> current Act, rules and latest judgments. Items marked **[VERIFY]** are from memory and must be
> checked (use the `legal-research-india` skill). Statutes in these areas are amended often.

## How to use a playbook

For a matter in any area:

1. Open the playbook file for the area (table below).
2. Check the **forum and limitation** first, then the **threshold requirements** (notices,
   sanctions, deposits, mandatory pre-litigation steps).
3. Use the **strategy** notes for the side you act for, and the **pitfalls** list as a checklist.
4. Verify the provisions and authorities you will rely on.
5. Hand over to the `senior-advocate` skill for case strategy and hearings, and to the
   `legal-drafting-india` skill for drafting.

## Cross-cutting checks in every area

- **Mandatory first steps:** a statutory notice (s.80 CPC, s.138 NI Act, s.8 IBC, s.13(2)
  SARFAESI), pre-institution mediation (s.12A Commercial Courts Act), a departmental remedy (service
  rules), or sanction to prosecute (s.218 BNSS; s.19 PC Act; s.17A PC Act).
- **Special forum bars:** the civil court is excluded where a special tribunal has exclusive
  jurisdiction (DRT, NCLT, RERA, KSAT, Labour Court, Consumer Commission is additional, not
  exclusive).
- **Pre-deposit for appeals:** many statutes require a deposit before an appeal is heard (for
  example s.18 SARFAESI, s.148 NI Act, s.43(5) RERA, GST, Consumer Protection Act). Budget for it.
- **Bail under special statutes:** twin conditions or bars (NDPS s.37, PMLA s.45, UAPA s.43D(5),
  SC/ST Act s.18 and s.18A). Prolonged custody and delay in trial are recognised grounds even under
  these statutes [VERIFY: *Union of India v. K.A. Najeeb*, (2021) 3 SCC 713].
- **Victims' identity** must never be disclosed in sexual offence and child cases (s.72 BNS; s.23
  POCSO).
