## 1. Consumer Protection Act, 2019

- **Pecuniary jurisdiction** (based on the consideration paid, not the compensation claimed): District
  Commission up to Rs. 50 lakh; State Commission above Rs. 50 lakh up to Rs. 2 crore; National
  Commission above Rs. 2 crore (Consumer Protection (Jurisdiction of the District Commission, the
  State Commission and the National Commission) Rules, 2021).
- **Territorial jurisdiction** includes where the complainant resides or personally works (s.34(2)(d)).
- **Limitation:** 2 years from the cause of action (s.69).
- **Appeals:** to the State Commission within 45 days with a deposit of 50% of the amount ordered
  (s.41); to the National Commission within 30 days (s.51); to the Supreme Court within 30 days
  (s.67).
- **Medical negligence:** medical services are "services" (*Indian Medical Association v. V.P.
  Shantha*, (1995) 6 SCC 651). For criminal liability of doctors, gross negligence is needed
  (*Jacob Mathew v. State of Punjab*, (2005) 6 SCC 1). Get an expert opinion before filing.
- **Common matters:** builders (also under RERA), insurance claim repudiation, banking services,
  education services (limited), e-commerce, airlines, and defective vehicles and goods.
- The consumer remedy is in addition to other remedies (s.100).

## 2. Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 (RERA)

- **Authority:** Karnataka Real Estate Regulatory Authority (K-RERA). Complaints under s.31.
  Compensation is decided by the Adjudicating Officer (s.71).
- **Key rights:** refund with interest on the promoter's failure to give possession (s.18(1)); interest
  for every month of delay if the allottee stays in the project (proviso to s.18(1)); the promoter
  cannot take more than 10% as an advance without a registered agreement (s.13).
- The allottee's right to a refund with interest on delay is unconditional, not dependent on
  contingencies (*Newtech Promoters and Developers v. State of U.P.*, (2021) 18 SCC 1) [VERIFY].
- **Appeals:** to the Karnataka Real Estate Appellate Tribunal within 60 days (s.44). A promoter
  appealing must deposit at least 30% of the penalty, or the full amount payable to the allottee
  (s.43(5)).
- **Execution:** recovery as arrears of land revenue (s.40). Non-compliance can be punished.
- **Choosing a forum:** RERA, the Consumer Commission or arbitration. RERA is often fastest for
  refunds; the Consumer Commission for compensation claims [VERIFY current practice in Karnataka].

## 3. Motor accident claims (Motor Vehicles Act, 1988)

- **Claim** to the Motor Accidents Claims Tribunal under s.166, within 6 months of the accident
  (s.166(3), inserted in 2019) [VERIFY how courts apply the limit].
- **No-fault and hit-and-run compensation** under the provisions as renumbered in 2019 (s.164 for
  fixed compensation; s.161 for hit-and-run) [VERIFY].
- **Computation in death cases:** the multiplier by age (*Sarla Verma v. DTC*, (2009) 6 SCC 121);
  future prospects, the deduction for personal expenses and conventional heads (*National Insurance
  Co. v. Pranay Sethi*, (2017) 16 SCC 680). Update conventional amounts as directed.
- **Injury cases:** medical expenses, loss of earnings, functional disability (assessed by a doctor
  and the tribunal), pain and suffering, future medical expenses, attendant charges.
- **Insurer's defences:** no valid driving licence, use outside the permit, the vehicle not insured,
  a gratuitous passenger in a goods vehicle. "Pay and recover" orders are common.
- **Appeal** to the High Court (MFA in Karnataka) under s.173 within 90 days, with the deposit the
  Act requires for the insurer or owner [VERIFY].

## 4. Insurance

- Repudiation must be on grounds in the policy, and suppression must be of a material fact. Delay in
  intimation alone does not defeat a genuine theft claim [VERIFY: *Gurshinder Singh v. Shriram
  General Insurance*, (2020) 11 SCC 612].
- **Forums:** Consumer Commission, the Insurance Ombudsman (for smaller claims), or a civil suit.
- Obtain the full policy with its terms and the proposal form, and the surveyor's report.

## 5. Labour and employment

  force on 21 November 2025, replacing 29 central labour laws [VERIFY the notification and the rules
  framed by Karnataka]. For disputes arising earlier, the old laws (Industrial Disputes Act, 1947,

- **Termination of a workman:** the conditions for retrenchment (notice, compensation, intimation),
  standing orders, and domestic inquiries. Raise the dispute before the conciliation officer, then
  the Industrial Tribunal [VERIFY the forum under the Industrial Relations Code].
- **Managerial staff** are generally not "workmen"; their remedies are under contract (damages).
- **Gratuity:** five years' continuous service, except on death or disablement.
- **Sexual harassment at the workplace:** the Internal Committee under the POSH Act, 2013; complaint
  within 3 months (extendable by 3 months).
