# Commercial, banking, insolvency and company law

Every section number and authority here is [VERIFY] unless marked ✓ (checked against the Supreme
Court judgments database on 19 November 2026).

## 1. Commercial Courts Act, 2015

- Applies to "commercial disputes" (s.2(1)(c)) of the **specified value** (at least Rs. 3 lakh since
  the 2018 amendment) [VERIFY].
- **Pre-institution mediation** under s.12A is mandatory unless urgent interim relief is sought
  (*Patil Automation*, [2022] 11 SCR 808 ✓). A suit filed without it is liable to be rejected.
  Whether the plea of urgency is genuine is examined from the plaint [VERIFY later decisions such as
  *Yamini Manohar v. T.K.D. Keerthi*, 2023].
- **Strict timelines:** written statement within 120 days at the outside; case management hearings;
  the CPC as amended for commercial suits (statement of truth, disclosure, denial with reasons).
- **Appeals** to the Commercial Appellate Division of the High Court within 60 days (s.13). No civil
  revision against interlocutory orders (s.8).
- **Summary judgment** under Order XIII-A where the defence has no real prospect of success.

## 2. Arbitration (Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996)

- **Before or during arbitration:** s.8 (refer the parties), s.9 (interim measures from the court),
  s.11 (appointment), s.16 (the tribunal rules on its own jurisdiction), s.17 (interim measures from
  the tribunal), s.29A (time limit for the award).
- **After the award:** s.34 (setting aside; 3 months plus 30 days at most), s.36 (enforcement; stay
  only on conditions), s.37 (appeals).
- **Leading authorities:** unstamped arbitration agreements (*In re Interplay*, [2023] 15 SCR 1081 :
  2023 INSC 1066 ✓, Seven-Judge Bench); limited power to modify an award (*Gayatri Balasamy*, [2025]
  4 SCR 2080 : 2025 INSC 605 ✓, Constitution Bench); arbitrability (*Vidya Drolia*, (2021) 2 SCC 1);
  the narrow grounds under s.34 after 2015 (*Ssangyong Engineering*, (2019) 15 SCC 131).
- **Strategy:** read the clause first (seat, venue, institution, number of arbitrators, any
  pre-arbitration steps). Move under s.9 quickly where assets may be dissipated. Keep the s.34
  challenge to the statutory grounds; a re-appreciation of evidence will fail.

## 3. Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC)

- **Corporate insolvency:** by a financial creditor (s.7), an operational creditor (s.9, after a
  demand notice under s.8), or the corporate debtor itself (s.10). The default threshold is Rs. 1
  crore [VERIFY].
- **Operational creditors:** a genuine pre-existing dispute defeats the application (*Mobilox
  Innovations v. Kirusa Software*, (2018) 1 SCC 353).
- **Constitutional validity** upheld (*Swiss Ribbons v. Union of India*, (2019) 4 SCC 17).
- **Moratorium** under s.14 on admission; s.29A ineligibility of resolution applicants.
- **Personal guarantors** can be proceeded against (*Lalit Kumar Jain v. Union of India*, (2021) 9
  SCC 321).
- **Forums:** NCLT (Bengaluru bench for Karnataka); appeal to the NCLAT within 30 days, extendable by
  15 days (s.61); appeal to the Supreme Court within 45 days, extendable by 15 days (s.62).
- **Strategy for the debtor:** settle before admission; raise a genuine dispute with documents
  created before the demand notice; check limitation (Art 137, three years from default, with
  acknowledgments in balance sheets extending it).

## 4. SARFAESI Act, 2002 and Debts Recovery Tribunals

- **Sequence:** the account is classified as an NPA; a demand notice under s.13(2) (60 days); the
  borrower's objection under s.13(3A) and the bank's reply within 15 days; measures under s.13(4)
  (possession, sale); the Magistrate's or DM's assistance under s.14.
- **Challenge:** a securitisation application to the DRT within 45 days (s.17); appeal to the DRAT
  within 30 days with a pre-deposit of 50% of the debt (reducible to 25%) (s.18) [VERIFY].
- **Writ petitions** are not ordinarily entertained because of this remedy (*United Bank of India v.
  Satyawati Tondon*, (2010) 8 SCC 110), except for a lack of jurisdiction or a statutory breach.
- The Act does not apply to agricultural land (s.31(i)) and to certain small loans.
- **Recovery suits** by banks for Rs. 20 lakh or more go to the DRT under the Recovery of Debts and
  Bankruptcy Act, 1993 [VERIFY the threshold].
- **Constitutional validity** upheld (*Mardia Chemicals v. Union of India*, (2004) 4 SCC 311).

## 5. Company law (Companies Act, 2013)

- **Oppression and mismanagement:** a petition to the NCLT under ss.241 and 242. Eligibility under
  s.244 (10% of members or of issued share capital, unless waived).
- **Appeals** to the NCLAT within 45 days (s.421) and to the Supreme Court within 60 days (s.423).
- **Winding up** now mostly through the IBC.
- **Directors' liability:** look for specific roles; non-executive and independent directors are
  generally not liable for the company's offences without specific involvement.

## 6. Money recovery and cheques

- **Options:** a summary suit (Order XXXVII CPC), a commercial suit, a s.138 NI Act complaint (with
  its pressure value), arbitration if there is a clause, an IBC application (if the threshold is
  met and there is no dispute), and the DRT for banks.
- **Choose by:** speed, the strength of documents, the debtor's assets, and the cost. Run the civil
  and criminal remedies in parallel where both lie.

## 7. Intellectual property (overview)

- **Trade marks** (Trade Marks Act, 1999): infringement (registered marks) and passing off (common
  law). Interim injunctions turn on prima facie case, delay and acquiescence, and the likelihood of
  confusion.
- **Copyright** (Copyright Act, 1957); **patents** (Patents Act, 1970); **designs** (Designs Act,
  2000).
- IP suits are commercial suits; the Commercial Division of the High Court or the Commercial Court
  hears them. Appeals that went to the IPAB now go to the High Courts (the IPAB was abolished in
  2021).
- **Strategy:** collect evidence of use and reputation (sales figures, advertising, invoices), move
  quickly (delay defeats interim relief), and consider an Anton Piller-type local commissioner to
  seize infringing goods.
