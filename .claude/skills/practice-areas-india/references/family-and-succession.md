# Family law and succession

Every section number and authority here is [VERIFY]: check the current text before relying on it.

## 1. Forums

- **Family Courts** (Family Courts Act, 1984) in Bengaluru and other districts hear matrimonial
  cases, maintenance, custody and guardianship. Where there is no Family Court, the District Court
  hears them. Lawyers appear with the court's permission in some Family Courts; check local practice.
- **Appeals** from a Family Court go to the High Court under s.19 of the Family Courts Act (30 days).
- **Magistrates** hear DV Act applications and s.144 BNSS maintenance where no Family Court has
  jurisdiction over it.
- **Transfer petitions** by a wife to the Supreme Court (inter-state) or the High Court are common.
  Video conferencing is an option [VERIFY current SC guidance].

## 2. Hindu Marriage Act, 1955

- **Grounds for divorce (s.13(1)):** adultery, cruelty, desertion for 2 years, conversion, mental
  disorder, communicable venereal disease, renunciation, not heard of for 7 years. Additional grounds
  for the wife (s.13(2)). No resumption of cohabitation for one year after a decree of judicial
  separation or restitution (s.13(1A)).
- **Mutual consent (s.13B):** living separately for one year; the second motion after six months
  (waivable: *Amardeep Singh v. Harveen Kaur*, (2017) 8 SCC 746).
- **Irretrievable breakdown** is not a ground under the HMA, but the Supreme Court can dissolve a
  marriage under Art 142 (*Shilpa Sailesh v. Varun Sreenivasan*, Constitution Bench, 1 May 2023).
- **Mental cruelty** is judged on the totality of conduct (*Samar Ghosh v. Jaya Ghosh*, (2007) 4
  SCC 511, with illustrative instances).
- **Interim maintenance and expenses** (s.24); **permanent alimony** (s.25); **custody** (s.26).
- **Bar on petitions within one year of marriage** (s.14), except for exceptional hardship.

## 3. Maintenance

- **s.144 BNSS (old s.125 CrPC):** wife (including a divorced wife who has not remarried), children
  and parents. Quick and summary; also for Muslim women (*Mohd. Abdul Samad v. State of Telangana*,
  2024) [VERIFY].
- **Guidelines on maintenance:** affidavits of assets and liabilities, the date from which
  maintenance is payable (the date of application), avoiding overlapping awards, the criteria for
  the amount, and enforcement (*Rajnesh v. Neha*, (2021) 2 SCC 324).
- Other sources: s.24 and s.25 HMA, s.18 Hindu Adoptions and Maintenance Act, s.20 DV Act. Set off
  amounts awarded in one proceeding in the others.

## 4. Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005

- An application under s.12 to the Magistrate. Reliefs: protection (s.18), residence (s.19),
  monetary (s.20), custody (s.21), compensation (s.22), interim and ex parte orders (s.23).
- **Shared household:** a woman can claim a right of residence in the shared household even if it
  belongs to her in-laws (*Satish Chander Ahuja v. Sneha Ahuja*, (2021) 1 SCC 414).
- Proceedings are civil in nature, but breach of a protection order is an offence (s.31).

## 5. Custody and guardianship

- The welfare of the child is paramount over the legal rights of either parent.
- **Statutes:** Guardians and Wards Act, 1890; Hindu Minority and Guardianship Act, 1956 (s.6 natural
  guardian; s.13 welfare is paramount).
- **Practical points:** the child's age (young children often stay with the mother), the child's
  wishes if mature, continuity of schooling, the home environment, and meaningful visitation for the
  other parent. Interview with the judge in chambers is common.
- **International custody:** the welfare principle prevails over comity; India is not a party to the
  Hague Convention on child abduction.

## 6. Other personal laws

- **Muslim law:** instant triple talaq is void and an offence (Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on
  Marriage) Act, 2019; *Shayara Bano v. Union of India*, (2017) 9 SCC 1). Mahr, maintenance during
  iddat, the Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Divorce) Act, 1986, and s.144 BNSS.
- **Christians:** Indian Divorce Act, 1869 (a one-year separation for mutual consent under s.10A).
- **Special Marriage Act, 1954:** inter-faith and civil marriages; notice of intended marriage; divorce
  under s.27 and s.28 (mutual consent).
- **Parsis:** Parsi Marriage and Divorce Act, 1936.

## 7. Succession

- **Hindu Succession Act, 1956:** daughters are coparceners by birth with the same rights as sons,
  whether or not the father was alive on 9 September 2005 (*Vineeta Sharma v. Rakesh Sharma*,
  (2020) 9 SCC 1). Class I heirs (s.8). A Hindu woman's property is her absolute property (s.14).
- **Wills:** proof by at least one attesting witness; suspicious circumstances must be dispelled by
  the propounder. Probate is not compulsory in Karnataka for Hindus [VERIFY].
- **Succession certificates** (Part X Indian Succession Act) for debts and securities; letters of
  administration where there is no will.
- **Muslim succession** is governed by personal law (Sunni and Shia rules differ). A Muslim can
  bequeath only one-third of the estate by will without the heirs' consent.

## 8. Strategy notes

- Matrimonial disputes usually involve several cases (divorce, maintenance, DV Act, s.85 BNS,
  custody). Plan them together, and aim for a global settlement covering all of them.
- Mediation is compulsory or strongly encouraged in Family Courts. Prepare the client for it.
- Keep allegations specific and provable. Exaggerated allegations damage credibility and can amount
  to cruelty themselves.
- Advise clients on interim maintenance exposure early.
