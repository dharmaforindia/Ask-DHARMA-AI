## 1. NDPS Act, 1985

- **Quantity decides everything:** small, intermediate and commercial quantity (as notified for each
  drug). Bail for commercial quantity is subject to the twin conditions in **s.37**: reasonable
  grounds to believe the accused is not guilty, and that they will not commit an offence on bail.
- **Search and seizure safeguards:** s.42 (recording information, sending it to the superior
  officer), **s.50** (the right of the person to be searched before a Gazetted Officer or
  Magistrate, applicable to personal search) (*State of Punjab v. Baldev Singh*, (1999) 6 SCC 172),
  **s.52A** (inventory, photographs and samples drawn in the presence of a Magistrate).
- **Confessional statements** to NDPS officers under s.67 are not admissible as confessions
  (*Tofan Singh v. State of Tamil Nadu*, (2021) 4 SCC 1).
- **Investigation period:** up to 180 days, extendable to one year on the Public Prosecutor's report
  (s.36A(4)). Oppose extensions not based on a proper report.
- **Defence:** non-compliance with s.42, s.50 or s.52A; delay in sending samples; mismatch in sample
  weights or seal numbers; the chain of custody; independent witnesses not joined; conscious
  possession not proved.
- **Prosecution:** prove conscious possession; presumption under ss.35 and 54 once possession is
  proved.

## 2. POCSO Act, 2012

- **Special Court** tries the case; in-camera trial; the child should not be called repeatedly to
  testify (s.33(5)); the child's identity must not be disclosed (s.23).
- **Presumptions:** s.29 (commission of the offence) and s.30 (culpable mental state) apply once the
  prosecution establishes the foundational facts.
- **Age** is decided under s.94 of the Juvenile Justice Act, 2015: school records first, then the
  birth certificate from the corporation or panchayat, then a medical age test.
- "Sexual assault" does not need skin-to-skin contact (*Attorney General for India v. Satish*, (2022)
  5 SCC 545).
- **Defence:** age not proved by the proper documents; contradictions between the s.183 BNSS
  statement, the medical history and the evidence; delay; motive; the medical evidence.
- Mandatory reporting under s.19 and s.21 (failure to report is an offence).

## 3. Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002 (PMLA)

- An offence under PMLA needs a **scheduled offence** and proceeds of crime. If the scheduled offence
  is quashed or the accused is acquitted or discharged of it, the PMLA case cannot stand
  (*Vijay Madanlal Choudhary v. Union of India*, 2022 SCC OnLine SC 929).
- **Bail:** twin conditions in s.45, with the proviso for women, minors and the sick or infirm.
- **Arrest under s.19:** the grounds of arrest must be given to the arrestee in writing (*Pankaj
  Bansal v. Union of India*, 2023 INSC 866).
- The ECIR is an internal document, not an FIR.
- Provisional attachment (s.5) goes to the Adjudicating Authority, then the Appellate Tribunal.

## 4. Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act, 1967 (UAPA)

- **Bail:** s.43D(5) bars bail if the accusation is prima facie true on the case diary and the
  charge-sheet. At the bail stage the court does not test the evidence in depth (*NIA v. Zahoor
  Ahmad Shah Watali*, (2019) 5 SCC 1). Prolonged custody with no early trial can justify bail
  despite s.43D(5) (*Union of India v. K.A. Najeeb*, (2021) 3 SCC 713).
- The grounds of arrest must be given in writing (*Prabir Purkayastha v. State (NCT of Delhi)*, 2024)
  [VERIFY citation].
- Investigation period up to 180 days on the Public Prosecutor's report (s.43D(2)). Sanction is
  required for prosecution (s.45).

## 5. SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989

- **Anticipatory bail is barred** (s.18 and s.18A), but the court can grant it where no prima facie
  case under the Act is made out on the complaint itself (*Prithvi Raj Chauhan v. Union of India*,
  (2020) 4 SCC 727).
- Key elements: the victim is SC/ST; the accused is not; the offence was committed **because** the
  victim belongs to an SC/ST (for certain offences) and/or **in public view** (for insults under
  s.3(1)(r) and (s)).
- **Appeals against bail orders** go to the High Court under s.14A, within 90 days.
- The victim has rights to notice and to be heard at bail and other stages (s.15A).

## 6. Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988

- **Sanction** to prosecute a public servant is required (s.19). **Prior approval** is required before
  investigating offences relating to recommendations or decisions taken in official functions
  (s.17A, inserted in 2018).
- **Trap cases:** demand and acceptance of a bribe must be proved. They may be proved by
  circumstantial evidence where direct evidence is not available (*Neeraj Dutta v. State (NCT of
  Delhi)*, (2023) 4 SCC 731, Constitution Bench).
- In Karnataka, investigations are by the Lokayukta police [VERIFY the current position after the
  ACB was abolished in 2022].
- **Defence:** no sanction or an invalid sanction (no application of mind); no proof of demand;
  hostile shadow witness; recovery without proof of demand; the money was a legitimate payment.

## 7. Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881: s.138

- Requirements: a cheque for a legally enforceable debt; presentation within validity; dishonour;
  demand notice within 30 days of being told; failure to pay within 15 days of the notice; a
  complaint within one month after that (s.142(1)(b)).
- **Presumption** under s.139 that the cheque was for a debt; rebuttable on a preponderance of
  probabilities (*Rangappa v. Sri Mohan*, [2010] 6 SCR 507 ✓).
- **Jurisdiction:** where the payee's bank branch (where the cheque was delivered for collection) is
  situated (s.142(2), after the 2015 amendment).
- **Companies:** directors are liable only with specific averments that they were in charge of and
  responsible for the business (s.141) (*S.M.S. Pharmaceuticals v. Neeta Bhalla*, (2005) 8 SCC 89).
- **Interim compensation** up to 20% at trial (s.143A) and a deposit of at least 20% in appeal (s.148).
- **Compounding** is encouraged at any stage (*Meters and Instruments v. Kanchan Mehta*, (2018) 1
  SCC 560).
- **Defence:** no legally enforceable debt (time-barred debt, security cheque with no liability then
  due, the amount filled in by someone else); notice defects; the complaint is premature or late; the
  financial capacity of the complainant to lend.

## 8. Information Technology Act, 2000 and cyber crime

- s.66A was struck down (*Shreya Singhal v. Union of India*, (2015) 5 SCC 1). Do not allow a
  prosecution under it.
- Common offences: s.43 read with s.66 (computer-related offences), s.66C (identity theft), s.66D
  (cheating by personation), s.66E (privacy), s.67, 67A and 67B (obscene material, child sexual
  material). The BNS covers cheating, extortion and defamation online.
- **Evidence:** a s.63 BSA (old s.65B) certificate for every electronic record; hash values; the
  chain of custody of devices; service provider records.
- **Intermediaries:** safe harbour under s.79, subject to due diligence and the IT Rules, 2021.
- Cyber crime complaints: the national portal (cybercrime.gov.in) and the CEN police stations in
  Karnataka [VERIFY].

## 9. Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015

- A "child in conflict with law" (under 18 on the date of the offence) is dealt with by the Juvenile
  Justice Board. Bail is the norm under s.12, unless release would expose the child to danger or
  defeat the ends of justice.
- For heinous offences by a child aged 16 to 18, the Board conducts a **preliminary assessment**
  (s.15) of mental and physical capacity before any transfer to the Children's Court.
- Raise the plea of juvenility at any stage, even after final disposal (s.9(2)).

## 10. Matrimonial cruelty (s.85 and s.86 BNS, old s.498A IPC) and dowry

- Arrest only after considering the need, with a notice under s.35(3) BNSS where the offence is
  punishable with up to 7 years (*Arnesh Kumar*, [2014] 8 SCR 128 ✓).
- **Quashing** against distant relatives with general and omnibus allegations is often granted.
  Look for specific allegations against each person.
- **Settlement:** quashing on compromise in matrimonial cases (*B.S. Joshi*, 13 March 2003 ✓).
- **Dowry death** (s.80 BNS, old s.304B IPC): death within 7 years of marriage, otherwise than in
  normal circumstances, with cruelty or harassment "soon before" death for dowry. The presumption is
  under s.118 BSA (old s.113B Evidence Act).
