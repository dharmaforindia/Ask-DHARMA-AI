## 1. Service law

- **Forums:** State government employees in Karnataka go to the **Karnataka State Administrative
  Tribunal (KSAT)**; Central government employees go to the **Central Administrative Tribunal (CAT)**,
  Bengaluru bench. Then a writ petition to a Division Bench of the High Court (*L. Chandra Kumar v.
  Union of India*, (1997) 3 SCC 261). Employees of PSUs, universities and other bodies go directly to
  the High Court under Art 226 unless a tribunal is notified for them.
- **Rules:** Art 309 (service rules), Art 311 (protection against dismissal without inquiry); the
  Karnataka Civil Services (Classification, Control and Appeal) Rules, 1957 and the Karnataka Civil
  Services Rules [VERIFY]; Central Civil Services (CCA) Rules, 1965.
- **Departmental inquiries:** the charge memo must be specific; the employee is entitled to the
  documents relied on, a reasonable opportunity, cross-examination of witnesses, and a copy of the
  inquiry report before the punishment order. Dispensing with an inquiry under Art 311(2)(b) needs
  recorded reasons (*Union of India v. Tulsiram Patel*, (1985) 3 SCC 398).
- **Judicial review** of punishment is limited to the decision-making process and to punishments that
  are shockingly disproportionate.
- **Regularisation:** no regularisation of irregular appointments as a matter of right (*Secretary,
  State of Karnataka v. Umadevi (3)*, (2006) 4 SCC 1), with the limited one-time exception it made.
- **Delay:** stale claims and fence-sitters may be denied relief, while continuing wrongs (pay
  fixation) can be corrected with arrears limited to a period [VERIFY: *State of U.P. v. Arvind
  Kumar Srivastava*, (2015) 1 SCC 347].
- **Common matters:** seniority, promotion, transfer (rarely interfered with unless mala fide or in
  breach of the policy), suspension (extended suspension without a charge-sheet), pension and
  retiral benefits, compassionate appointment (not a right; governed by the scheme).

## 2. Tax

- **GST:** an appeal to the Appellate Authority within 3 months (plus 1 month on sufficient cause)
  with a pre-deposit of 10% of the disputed tax (s.107 CGST Act). The GST Appellate Tribunal
  [VERIFY its operational status and benches]. Writ petitions lie for breaches of natural justice,
  lack of jurisdiction (for example, a show-cause notice beyond the time limit), or the vires of a
  provision; otherwise the High Court sends the petitioner to the appeal.
- **Income tax:** the Income-tax Act, 1961 was replaced by the **Income-tax Act, 2025** with effect
  from 1 April 2026 [VERIFY the commencement and transition provisions]. For earlier years, the 1961
  Act applies. Appeals go to the Commissioner (Appeals), then the ITAT, then the High Court on a
  substantial question of law. Reassessment notices have been the subject of major litigation
  (*Union of India v. Ashish Agarwal*, (2023) 1 SCC 617; *Union of India v. Rajeev Bansal*, 2024)
  [VERIFY].
- **Strategy:** in writ challenges to tax notices, identify a jurisdictional defect or a breach of
  natural justice. Do not ask the High Court to decide disputed facts that the appeal remedy covers.

## 3. Right to Information Act, 2005

- Application under s.6; reply in 30 days (48 hours for life and liberty) (s.7). First appeal within 30
  days (s.19(1)). Second appeal to the Karnataka Information Commission (or the Central Information
  Commission for central bodies) within 90 days (s.19(3)).
- **Exemptions:** s.8 (including personal information in s.8(1)(j), as amended by the Digital
  Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 [VERIFY the commencement]), s.9, s.24 (certain intelligence and
  security organisations).
- RTI is a strong tool to collect documents for litigation (file notings, inspection reports,
  service records).

## 4. Election law

- **Parliament and State Legislature:** an election petition to the High Court within 45 days of the
  result (s.81 RP Act). Material facts and full particulars of any corrupt practice must be pleaded
  (s.83), with an affidavit in Form 25 for corrupt practices. Grounds: s.100. Corrupt practices:
  s.123.
- **Local bodies:** under the Karnataka Panchayat Raj Act, 1993 and the municipal laws, election
  petitions go to the designated court (usually a civil judge or district judge) [VERIFY].
- **Disqualification and anti-defection:** the Tenth Schedule; the Speaker's decisions are subject to
  limited judicial review (*Kihoto Hollohan v. Zachillhu*, 1992 Supp (2) SCC 651).

## 5. Environment

- **National Green Tribunal (NGT):** the Southern Zone bench at Chennai covers Karnataka. Applications
  under s.14 (substantial questions relating to the environment) and s.15 (relief and compensation)
  within 6 months of the cause of action, extendable by 60 days; appeals under s.16 within 30 days
  (extendable by 60 days); appeal to the Supreme Court within 90 days (s.22).
- **Principles:** polluter pays, precautionary principle, sustainable development, public trust.
- **Karnataka matters:** lake and tank-bed encroachment, forest clearance, mining, the Western Ghats,
  buffer zones, and the Karnataka State Pollution Control Board's consent orders.

## 6. Education

- Admissions (merit, reservation, domicile), fee regulation, recognition and affiliation, and student
  discipline. Minority institutions' rights under Art 30 (*T.M.A. Pai Foundation*, (2002) 8 SCC 481).
- Bodies: Karnataka Examinations Authority, universities under the Karnataka State Universities Act,
  the UGC, the NMC, the AICTE.
- Courts interfere with academic decisions only for illegality, arbitrariness or mala fides.
